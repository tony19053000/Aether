Project Overview

About

From recorded footage to reviewable events

Aether is a video-based fall detection prototype. It analyzes human movement in uploaded footage, flags potential falls, and returns an annotated video alongside a detection summary and timeline. Its purpose is to help a person review possible incidents more efficiently.

1. Upload2. Analyze motion3. Review results

The problem it addresses

Reviewing footage manually takes time. A fall can occur within seconds, and a single image does not capture how the movement developed. Aether examines a sequence of body positions to identify motion patterns associated with falls.

How the analysis works

  1. Read the video: OpenCV processes the footage frame by frame.
  2. Extract body landmarks: MediaPipe Pose supplies 17 selected landmarks, each represented by horizontal position, vertical position, and visibility: 51 features per frame.
  3. Normalize movement: Positions are centered around the hips and scaled using the shoulder-to-hip distance when reliable landmarks are available.
  4. Classify a sequence: A TensorFlow Lite Transformer model analyzes a sliding window of 30 frames. A fall probability of 0.90 or above is classified as a fall.
  5. Present the evidence: The output video shows the skeleton and predictions. A 10-second cooldown limits repeated fall-event messages; periodic predictions still appear in the timeline.

What you can demonstrate

  • Upload a video, or choose one of four samples: a warehouse fall, a gym walking fall, a fall from a chair, and indoor walking without a fall.
  • Click Analyze Video and watch the annotated result play on repeat. Use Stop Video to stop playback.
  • Review the outcome, video timestamps, and model confidence. Peak model confidence is the highest score among the displayed predictions for the reported outcome; it is not a measure of overall accuracy.
  • Use Download Results to save the text summary, or Reset to begin another demonstration.

Technology and implementation

Interface: HTML, Tailwind CSS, and JavaScript. Server: Python and FastAPI. Vision and inference: OpenCV, MediaPipe Pose, and TensorFlow Lite. FFmpeg converts the annotated output for browser playback. The repository also contains Gradio tooling and notebooks for LSTM, bidirectional LSTM, and Transformer experiments.

In this local setup, uploaded video analysis runs on the local server. The page loads some styling and fonts from external services.

Current scope and next steps

This version processes uploaded videos; it does not connect to live cameras, send notifications, or provide multi-person tracking. The model can miss falls or produce false alarms, especially with occlusion, poor lighting, or unfamiliar motion. Results support human review and do not establish that a scene is safe.

The displayed timeline and downloaded summary contain the latest 15 status entries, not a complete event history. Future work could include broader evaluation, complete event storage, live video input, and alert delivery.

A short presentation introduction

“Aether turns recorded video into a visual review of potential falls. Instead of relying on one image, it tracks body landmarks across a sequence of frames. The result is an annotated video and a readable timeline, so the viewer can inspect what the model detected. This prototype demonstrates the complete path from video input to reviewable output.”
